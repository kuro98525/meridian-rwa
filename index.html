The Deep Dive

What actually happens when you tokenize a treasury

You're not tokenizing paper. You're tokenizing a claim.

Take a U.S. Treasury. It isn't scanned into a JPEG. Instead, the ownership interest is wrapped in a legal structure — a fund or a trust — and the claim on it is recorded on-chain as a token. The underlying asset stays with a regulated custodian. The token is your receipt, your entitlement, and your exit.

The change isn't the asset. It's the clock.

Traditional settlement runs on T+1 — a day of lag, and weeks of paperwork for private credit or real estate. On-chain, you buy at 2am and it settles in seconds. Same yield, same underlying, but you decide when to move — not a counterparty's office hours.

Liquidity is the product.

Private credit and real estate were never "bad" assets — they were illiquid assets. You could own them for a decade with no way out. Tokenization doesn't make them risk-free; it makes them movable. That's the entire pitch, and it's why institutions — not retail — moved first.

What tokenization does — and doesn't — change.

  • It changes access and settlement, not the underlying risk. A treasury is still a treasury; private credit still has default risk.
  • Regulatory, custody and counterparty risk remain. This is a new rail, not a new asset class.
  • These are income / yield assets, not moonshots. If you're looking for crypto-style upside, this isn't that.
  • For accredited / sophisticated investors only.
The Questions

Asked, answered.

What actually gets tokenized?

The ownership interest — the claim on the asset — not the physical asset itself. The underlying stays with a regulated custodian.

Is it safe? Who has custody?

Regulated, licensed custodians hold the underlying assets. The token is the record of your claim.

How liquid is private credit on-chain?

Historically illiquid. On-chain, the same exposure becomes divisible and tradable — that's the entire point.

Who can access it?

Accredited / sophisticated investors only. Access is private.

What are the risks?

Regulatory, counterparty and illiquidity risk remain. Tokenization changes access, not the underlying risk.

Do I need to be crypto-native?

No. Custody and rails are abstracted away — you hold a familiar asset class, on a faster rail.
